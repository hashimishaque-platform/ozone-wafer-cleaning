# Roadmap

Each step adds one piece, with its sources and a check, and keeps the browser page and the Python
package in step.

- [ ] The decay at 50 to 150 mg/L. The rate law gives much shorter half-lives there than tool
      makers quote (Figure 7b); stage 1 of the test plan measures them, and k₂ is refitted if
      needed. This is the largest open question.
- [ ] The second ring of nozzles and the cover gas of the closed-loop machine in the browser
      version, so the machine can be tried on the page like the rig.
- [ ] A rotor-stator variant of the machine: a cover that spins a few millimetres above the wafer,
      from the transfer data of rotor-stator spinning-disc reactors (Meeuwse et al. 2010).
- [ ] Cost per wafer from the use per wafer: ultrapure water, oxygen, electricity and exhaust at
      local prices, next to single-wafer SPM.
- [ ] A nozzle that swings across the wafer, so the strip rate becomes a time average, and the
      uniformity it gives.
- [ ] A heated wafer under cooler water: temperature across the film and its effect on decay,
      diffusion and solubility.
- [ ] The film near the centre, where inertia matters, from published asymptotic solutions, and a
      correction for waves.
- [ ] Degassing of supersaturated water at the nozzle, instead of only a warning.
- [ ] Radical scavengers in the decay law (acetic acid, and the organic acids the resist turns
      into), where rate constants are published.
- [ ] Uncertainty bands from the spread of the literature values (Henry's law, decay, diffusivity).
- [ ] Calibration from several measured points: the yield together with a surface rate constant
      and its activation energy.
- [ ] Megasonic mass transfer from a bubble dynamics model instead of a fixed factor.
- [ ] Immersion tanks and sprays.
- [ ] UV-assisted chemistry (UV/O₃ and UV/H₂O₂), with photon flux and absorption in the film,
      for the mercury-free UV option of the machine (222 nm lamps or UVC LEDs).
- [ ] The square shape of a photomask.
- [ ] Temperature-dependent rate constants in the radical mechanism, where they are published.
