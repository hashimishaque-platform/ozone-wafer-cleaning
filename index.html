Ozonated water for photoresist removal: a process model

This program models a single-wafer process in which ozonated water (DIO3) strips photoresist from a spinning wafer. It follows the ozone from the generator gas into the water, through the supply line and across the liquid film on the wafer into the resist. It shows how fast the resist strips across the wafer, where the ozone goes, and how temperature, spin speed, flow, pH and the gas above the film change the result. Choose an example or enter your own values and press Compute. The last part designs a test rig and a test plan to check the model on real wafers.

About the data. This is a theoretical model built from published equations and data, each cited below. The checks compare it with published values and with exact solutions. How much resist a given amount of ozone removes depends on the resist, so that one number is calibrated against published strip rates; with your own measurements you can calibrate it in the Resist box. No measured data, and no data from any research centre or company process, were used.

Chemistry

Solubility

Ozone dissolves in water in proportion to its partial pressure (Henry's law). At 25 °C water holds 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/m³ for every pascal of ozone partial pressure, and about twice as much at 5 °C (Burkholder et al. 2019, as listed by Sander 2023):

c = H(T) · y · p,   H(298 K) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol m⁻³ Pa⁻¹,   d ln H / d(1/T) = 2800 K

where y is the ozone mole fraction in the gas and p the total pressure. Individual measurements scatter by about 20 % around this value. Gas with 200 g/Nm³ of ozone in oxygen (13 wt %) at 1 atm gives about 53 mg/L at 20 °C. A higher contactor pressure raises this in proportion; warmer water holds less.

Decay

Dissolved ozone decomposes on its own. In acidic water the decay is slow and first order: De Smedt et al. (2002) measured a half-life of 3 to 4 h at pH 1.8 to 3 and room temperature, with an activation energy of 39.6 kJ/mol. Near neutral pH hydroxide ions start a radical chain, and the decay becomes faster and of higher order in ozone (De Smedt et al. 2002; Gurol and Singer 1982). The program uses

−d[O₃]/dt = k₁[O₃] + k₂[O₃]²,   k₂ = 10−2.66 + 0.49 pH L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹ at 19 °C

with k₁ from De Smedt et al. and k₂ and its activation energy of 76 kJ/mol from Ershov and Morozov (2009). The two sources together cover pH 2 to 8.

The radical chain

Figure 6 compares this rate law with the Staehelin-Hoigné-Bühler mechanism (Staehelin and Hoigné 1982; Bühler et al. 1984), using the rate constants compiled by Lesko (2004) and two from Buxton et al. (1988). Hydroxide ions start the chain (O₃ + OH⁻ → HO₂⁻ + O₂). Hydroperoxide reacts with ozone to give an OH radical and superoxide. Superoxide turns ozone into the ozonide radical, which takes up a proton and falls apart into another OH radical, and OH radicals destroy more ozone. Three consequences can be read from Figure 6:

  • A lower pH slows the chain, which is one reason CO₂ is added to ultrapure water.
  • Carbonate from dissolved CO₂ captures OH radicals and makes ozone last longer.
  • Traces of hydrogen peroxide start the chain much faster, so ozone disappears within seconds to minutes.

The mechanism agrees with the rate law only when the water holds about as much carbonate as water in contact with air. In perfectly degassed water it predicts a much shorter life.

Resist and silicon

Ozone breaks the resist into small organic acids: De Smedt et al. (2002) found acetate, formate, pyruvate, glyoxylate, oxalate and ketomalonate from an i-line resist. i-line resists strip faster than KrF resists, and ArF resists, which contain no phenol, slowest (Abe et al. 2003). On bare silicon, ozonated water grows a chemical oxide of 0.5 to 1.2 nm within 5 to 10 min even at a few mg/L; above about 20 mg/L more ozone does not make it thicker, and temperature between 20 and 50 °C changes it little (De Smedt et al. 2002). So the resist can be stripped without consuming silicon. Oxidation does not lift particles; they need a separate step.

Engineering

Supply

An ozone generator makes ozone from oxygen; generators reach about 300 g/m³, almost 15 % by volume (De Smedt et al. 2002). A contactor dissolves it into ultrapure water. The program assumes that the contactor reaches a chosen fraction of Henry's-law equilibrium. On the way to the nozzle the water is at the process temperature and ozone decays. If the water is warmer at the nozzle than in the contactor, it holds more ozone than it can keep at 1 atm. It is then supersaturated and loses ozone as bubbles or through its surface; the results table gives the ratio.

The film on the spinning wafer

A nozzle at the centre feeds the water onto the spinning wafer. Away from the centre the film is thin, and the centrifugal force balances viscous shear (Emslie, Bonner and Peck 1958):

h(r) = (3νQ / 2πω²r²)1/3,   u(r, z) = (ω²r / ν)(hz − z²/2)

At 500 rpm and 1.5 L/min the film is about 90 µm thick at mid-radius of a 300 mm wafer. Near the centre inertia matters and real films are thicker; waves can make films up to about 40 % thicker than this (Bhatelia et al. 2009). The results table gives the ratio of inertial to viscous forces at mid-radius; it should be well below 1.

Ozone transport through the film

To react, ozone has to diffuse across the film to the resist. The program solves the convection and diffusion of ozone in the film, with decay in the water, consumption at the resist and exchange with the gas above. Because the velocity profile has the same shape at every radius, the streamlines lie at fixed fractions of the film thickness. The film is split into 48 stream tubes, and the equations are marched outward from the nozzle; the ozone balance closes to rounding error. The ozone diffusivity is 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ exp(−1896 K/T) m²/s (Johnson and Davis 1996), about 1.9 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s at 25 °C.

At the resist the default assumes every ozone molecule that arrives reacts: published work finds the strip rate limited by ozone transport, rising with concentration, spin speed and agitation (Gottschalk and Schweckendiek 2004; Chen 2002). At the free surface the water is in equilibrium with the gas above it. Faster spinning thins the film and both boundary layers, so more ozone reaches the resist, but more also escapes.

The gas above the film

With fresh air above the film a large part of the ozone escapes before it can react. Filling the chamber with ozone-containing gas keeps the surface of the film near saturation, so ozone diffuses through a thin, hot film to the resist instead of escaping. This is the idea behind processes that combine a thin water film with ozone gas (Bergman and Hess, US 6,267,125). Choose "process gas" above to see the effect.

Megasonics

Agitation thins the diffusion layer at the resist (Chen 2002). Dussault, Fittkau and Gottschalk measured strip rates over 65 % higher with megasonics than without. The program represents this with a single enhancement factor on the ozone diffusivity in the film.

Process

Temperature has two opposite effects. Warmer water carries ozone to the resist faster, but it holds less ozone and loses it faster (Gottschalk and Schweckendiek 2004). At a fixed nozzle concentration the strip rate rises with temperature. When the ozone is dissolved cold and the water is heated on the way, the concentration at the nozzle falls as the temperature rises, so there is a best temperature (Figure 5).

Uniformity. With a fixed nozzle at the centre, the centre strips faster than the edge. A nozzle that swings across the wafer evens this out; that is not modelled yet. The time to clear the resist is set by the slowest point.

Ozone use and safety. The results table gives the ozone used per wafer and the share of it that reaches the resist. Ozone that escapes from the film, and the undissolved ozone from the contactor, must go through an ozone destruct unit. For comparison, the OSHA limit for workplace air is 0.1 ppm averaged over 8 h, the NIOSH ceiling is 0.1 ppm, and 5 ppm is immediately dangerous to life or health (NIOSH). The exhaust concentration on the page assumes the released ozone mixes into the stated exhaust flow.

Photomasks. Mask cleaning has moved towards sulfate-free chemistry, because sulfate left by sulfuric-acid cleans can grow haze under 193 nm exposure (Proc. SPIE 6283, 2006); ozonated water is one of the sulfate-free options. The program treats a 6-inch mask as a disc of 152 mm; the corners of a square mask are not modelled.

Calibration

The strip rate is the ozone flux into the resist multiplied by the resist volume removed per mole of ozone. That yield depends on the resist. Three published room-temperature results give it once the settings they do not report are assumed: Abe et al. (2003) report about 100 nm/min with 10 to 30 ppm ozone water; the NEC patent US 6,559,064 reports about 300 nm/min with 100 ppm on a rotating wafer; Dussault, Fittkau and Gottschalk report 279 nm/min at 80 ppm, 20 °C, 110 rpm and 1.4 L/min with megasonics, which raised the rate by over 65 %. With a 200 mm wafer and typical spin settings these imply 0.8 to 1.6 g of ozone per gram of resist (table below). The default is 1.0 g/g. For your own resist, enter a measured mean strip rate and press "Calibrate to this rate".

Test rig

The model can be checked on a bench built around one spin chamber (Figure 7). The rig measures every number the model uses or predicts: the ozone in the generator gas; the dissolved ozone after the contactor, before the nozzle and in the drain; the ozone that escapes into the exhaust; the temperatures, flows and spin speed; and the resist thickness before and after each run. A production tool needs few of these sensors. The rig needs them all, so that each part of the model can be tested on its own and a disagreement points to one part.

Ozone gas from the generator dissolves in a membrane contactor. A back-pressure regulator on the gas outlet sets the contactor pressure, and a chiller sets the water temperature there. The water then passes a flow controller, a heater that sets the process temperature, a bubble trap and a supply line of known length on its way to a fixed nozzle above the centre of the wafer. A three-way valve sends it to the drain until the ozone reading before the nozzle is steady. Ozone gas can also be fed into the chamber lid, which is the "process gas" setting of the model. Everything that can carry ozone gas ends in the destruct unit.

Figure 7. Piping and instruments of the test rig. Solid lines carry water, dashed lines gas. The circles are instruments: AIT-102 measures the ozone in the generator gas, AIT-202 the resistivity of the water (which gives its pH), AIT-204 and AIT-207 the dissolved ozone after the contactor and before the nozzle, AIT-302 the dissolved ozone in the drain, FT-303 and AIT-304 the flow and ozone in the exhaust, and AIT-401 the ozone in the room.

Measurements

Dissolved ozone is read by UV absorption near 260 nm, where ozone in water absorbs with 3000 L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ (Levanov et al. 2016). The cell path is chosen so the absorbance stays between about 0.1 and 1; the sizing table gives it for a 1 mm cell. The monitors are checked against the indigo method (Bader and Hoigné 1981), a wet-chemical method made for ozone in water.

pH. Glass electrodes drift in ultrapure water, so the rig reads resistivity, corrected to 25 °C as such meters do, and converts it to pH with the CO₂ balance of the Chemistry section. Ultrapure water reads 18.2 MΩ cm (Light et al. 2005), water in contact with air about 1 MΩ cm (pH 5.6). The sizing table gives the reading for the pH in the form.

Strip rate. The resist is measured at the same 49 points before and after a run. The clock starts when the ozonated water reaches the wafer and stops with a rinse in ozone-free water. The change in thickness over the time gives the strip rate at each radius, to compare with Figure 1.

Ozone budget. The ozone at the nozzle (AIT-207) times the flow is what goes in. The ozone in the drain (AIT-302) times the flow is what runs off the edge; it is a lower bound, because the droplets lose some ozone in the bowl. The exhaust flow times the ozone in it (FT-303 and AIT-304) is what escapes to the gas, after the background without water is taken off. Runs with and without resist then give the share that reaches the resist, so each bar of Figure 2 can be measured.

Solubility should agree with Henry's law within about 20 %, the scatter of the published measurements. In stage 3 only one number is fitted, the yield, and only at the centre point; the model then has to predict the other runs. A fair target is the mean strip rate within about 20 % and the same order of the effects. If the mean is right but the profile is not, the film flow or the gas exchange is at fault. If twice the ozone does not give twice the rate, the resist is not transport-limited, and a surface rate constant has to be fitted as well (the Python package has one).

Stage 3 is a two-level design in five factors. Sixteen runs, half of the full design (resolution V), separate all five effects and all interactions between two factors; four centre points show curvature and give the repeatability. In these runs the contactor works at the process temperature, so the water at the nozzle is not supersaturated; the sweep with cold-dissolved ozone follows separately. Run the rows in random order. Stop each run before the fastest point clears, so the whole profile can be measured: the last column gives 60 % of the predicted time for the fastest point outside the nozzle zone. The predictions take every other setting from the form.

Safety and materials

  • Parts that touch ozone water are made of PFA, PTFE or PVDF, with seals of perfluoroelastomer (FFKM); many other plastics and elastomers degrade in concentrated ozone. Gas lines are PFA or electropolished stainless steel, cleaned for oxygen service, with no oil or grease.
  • A check valve, a liquid trap and a relief valve keep water and pressure away from the generator.
  • All ozone gas goes to the destruct unit: the contactor off-gas, the bubble-trap vent and the chamber exhaust. The drain water still carries ozone (sizing table), so it needs time to decay, or a vented tank, before it reaches the plant drain.
  • Ambient monitors sit at the chamber and at the generator. The interlock (I in Figure 7) stops the generator and shuts the valve to the nozzle at 0.1 ppm, which is the OSHA limit averaged over 8 h and the NIOSH ceiling, and also on low exhaust flow, an open lid, a leak in the drip tray or lost cooling water. Before the lid opens, the chamber is purged until AIT-304 reads close to zero.
  • Equipment for fabs is assessed against SEMI S2, the environmental, health and safety guideline for semiconductor manufacturing equipment; a laboratory rig should follow the same ideas.

Checks

The Python version of this model (in the repository) runs the same checks as tests.

Limitations

  • The film is a steady, smooth, laminar film; inertia near the centre and waves are left out, and the zone under the nozzle (r < 10 mm) is not modelled.
  • Water and wafer are at one temperature; a heated wafer under cooler water is not modelled.
  • The nozzle is fixed at the centre; swinging nozzles, sprays and immersion are not modelled.
  • The decay law covers pH 2 to 8 and about 10 to 70 °C; organic acids from the resist, which can scavenge radicals, are ignored.
  • One calibrated yield describes the resist; implanted or hard-baked resists strip differently, and no surface reaction limit is applied by default.
  • The contactor efficiency is set by hand; bubble formation in supersaturated water is flagged but not modelled.
  • UV-assisted ozone and peroxide chemistry is not included.
