Ozonated water for photoresist removal: a process model

This program models a single-wafer process in which ozonated water (DIO3) strips photoresist from a spinning wafer. It follows the ozone from the generator gas into the water, through the supply line and across the liquid film on the wafer into the resist. It shows how fast the resist strips across the wafer, where the ozone goes, and how temperature, spin speed, flow, pH and the gas above the film change the result. Choose an example or enter your own values and press Compute. Later sections check the model against published measurements, design a test rig to test it on real wafers, with its mechanical drawings, and design a closed-loop machine that uses less water, ozone and energy.

About the data. This is a theoretical model built from published equations and data, each cited below. The checks compare it with published values and with exact solutions. How much resist a given amount of ozone removes depends on the resist, so that one number is calibrated against published strip rates; with your own measurements you can calibrate it in the Resist box. No measured data, and no data from any research centre or company process, were used.

Chemistry

Solubility

Ozone dissolves in water in proportion to its partial pressure (Henry's law). At 25 °C water holds 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/m³ for every pascal of ozone partial pressure, and about twice as much at 5 °C (Burkholder et al. 2019, as listed by Sander 2023):

c = H(T) · y · p,   H(298 K) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol m⁻³ Pa⁻¹,   d ln H / d(1/T) = 2800 K

where y is the ozone mole fraction in the gas and p the total pressure. Individual measurements scatter by about 20 % around this value. Gas with 200 g/Nm³ of ozone in oxygen (13 wt %) at 1 atm gives about 53 mg/L at 20 °C. A higher contactor pressure raises this in proportion; warmer water holds less.

Decay

Dissolved ozone decomposes on its own. In acidic water the decay is slow and first order: De Smedt et al. (2002) measured a half-life of 3 to 4 h at pH 1.8 to 3 and room temperature, with an activation energy of 39.6 kJ/mol. Near neutral pH hydroxide ions start a radical chain, and the decay becomes faster and of higher order in ozone (De Smedt et al. 2002; Gurol and Singer 1982). The program uses

−d[O₃]/dt = k₁[O₃] + k₂[O₃]²,   k₂ = 10−2.66 + 0.49 pH L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹ at 19 °C

with k₁ from De Smedt et al. and k₂ and its activation energy of 76 kJ/mol from Ershov and Morozov (2009). The two sources together cover pH 2 to 8.

The radical chain

Figure 6 compares this rate law with the Staehelin-Hoigné-Bühler mechanism (Staehelin and Hoigné 1982; Bühler et al. 1984), using the rate constants compiled by Lesko (2004) and two from Buxton et al. (1988). Hydroxide ions start the chain (O₃ + OH⁻ → HO₂⁻ + O₂). Hydroperoxide reacts with ozone to give an OH radical and superoxide. Superoxide turns ozone into the ozonide radical, which takes up a proton and falls apart into another OH radical, and OH radicals destroy more ozone. Three consequences can be read from Figure 6:

  • A lower pH slows the chain, which is one reason CO₂ is added to ultrapure water.
  • Carbonate from dissolved CO₂ captures OH radicals and makes ozone last longer.
  • Traces of hydrogen peroxide start the chain much faster, so ozone disappears within seconds to minutes.

The mechanism agrees with the rate law only when the water holds about as much carbonate as water in contact with air. In perfectly degassed water it predicts a much shorter life.

Resist and silicon

Ozone breaks the resist into small organic acids: De Smedt et al. (2002) found acetate, formate, pyruvate, glyoxylate, oxalate and ketomalonate from an i-line resist. i-line resists strip faster than KrF resists, and ArF resists, which contain no phenol, slowest (Abe et al. 2003). On bare silicon, ozonated water grows a chemical oxide of 0.5 to 1.2 nm within 5 to 10 min even at a few mg/L; above about 20 mg/L more ozone does not make it thicker, and temperature between 20 and 50 °C changes it little (De Smedt et al. 2002). So the resist can be stripped without consuming silicon. Oxidation does not lift particles; they need a separate step.

Engineering

Supply

An ozone generator makes ozone from oxygen; generators reach about 300 g/m³, almost 15 % by volume (De Smedt et al. 2002). A contactor dissolves it into ultrapure water. The program assumes that the contactor reaches a chosen fraction of Henry's-law equilibrium. On the way to the nozzle the water is at the process temperature and ozone decays. If the water is warmer at the nozzle than in the contactor, it holds more ozone than it can keep at 1 atm. It is then supersaturated and loses ozone as bubbles or through its surface; the results table gives the ratio.

The film on the spinning wafer

A nozzle at the centre feeds the water onto the spinning wafer. Away from the centre the film is thin, and the centrifugal force balances viscous shear (Emslie, Bonner and Peck 1958):

h(r) = (3νQ / 2πω²r²)1/3,   u(r, z) = (ω²r / ν)(hz − z²/2)

At 500 rpm and 1.5 L/min the film is about 90 µm thick at mid-radius of a 300 mm wafer. Near the centre inertia matters and real films are thicker; waves can make films up to about 40 % thicker than this (Bhatelia et al. 2009). The results table gives the ratio of inertial to viscous forces at mid-radius; it should be well below 1.

Ozone transport through the film

To react, ozone has to diffuse across the film to the resist. The program solves the convection and diffusion of ozone in the film, with decay in the water, consumption at the resist and exchange with the gas above. Because the velocity profile has the same shape at every radius, the streamlines lie at fixed fractions of the film thickness. The film is split into 48 stream tubes, and the equations are marched outward from the nozzle; the ozone balance closes to rounding error. The ozone diffusivity is 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ exp(−1896 K/T) m²/s (Johnson and Davis 1996), about 1.9 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s at 25 °C.

At the resist the default assumes every ozone molecule that arrives reacts: published work finds the strip rate limited by ozone transport, rising with concentration, spin speed and agitation (Gottschalk and Schweckendiek 2004; Chen 2002). At the free surface the water is in equilibrium with the gas above it. Faster spinning thins the film and both boundary layers, so more ozone reaches the resist, but more also escapes.

The gas above the film

With fresh air above the film a large part of the ozone escapes before it can react. Filling the chamber with ozone-containing gas keeps the surface of the film near saturation, so ozone diffuses through a thin, hot film to the resist instead of escaping. This is the idea behind processes that combine a thin water film with ozone gas (Bergman and Hess, US 6,267,125). Choose "process gas" above to see the effect.

Megasonics

Agitation thins the diffusion layer at the resist (Chen 2002). Dussault, Fittkau and Gottschalk (2014) measured strip rates of a positive resist over 65 % higher with megasonics than without, and found that more megasonic power gave a lower rate. The program represents agitation with a single enhancement factor on the ozone diffusivity in the film.

Process

Temperature has two opposite effects. Warmer water carries ozone to the resist faster, but it holds less ozone and loses it faster (Gottschalk and Schweckendiek 2004). At a fixed nozzle concentration the strip rate rises with temperature. When the ozone is dissolved cold and the water is heated on the way, the concentration at the nozzle falls as the temperature rises, so there is a best temperature (Figure 5).

Uniformity. With a fixed nozzle at the centre, the centre strips faster than the edge. A nozzle that swings across the wafer evens this out; that is not modelled yet. The time to clear the resist is set by the slowest point.

Ozone use and safety. The results table gives the ozone used per wafer and the share of it that reaches the resist. Ozone that escapes from the film, and the undissolved ozone from the contactor, must go through an ozone destruct unit. For comparison, the OSHA limit for workplace air is 0.1 ppm averaged over 8 h, the NIOSH ceiling is 0.1 ppm, and 5 ppm is immediately dangerous to life or health (NIOSH). The exhaust concentration on the page assumes the released ozone mixes into the stated exhaust flow.

Photomasks. Mask cleaning has moved towards sulfate-free chemistry, because sulfate left by sulfuric-acid cleans can grow haze under 193 nm exposure (Proc. SPIE 6283, 2006); ozonated water is one of the sulfate-free options. The program treats a 6-inch mask as a disc of 152 mm; the corners of a square mask are not modelled.

Calibration

The strip rate is the ozone flux into the resist multiplied by the resist volume removed per mole of ozone. That yield depends on the resist. Three published room-temperature results give it once the settings they do not report are assumed: Abe et al. (2003) report about 100 nm/min with 10 to 30 ppm ozone water; the NEC patent US 6,559,064 reports about 300 nm/min with 100 ppm on a rotating wafer; Dussault, Fittkau and Gottschalk (2014) report 279 nm/min for a negative resist at 80 ppm, 20 °C, 110 rpm and 1.4 L/min with megasonics, the water fed through a radial manifold under the transducer. The megasonic gain of 65 % that is taken out of their value was measured on a positive resist, so this point is the weakest of the three. With a 200 mm wafer and typical spin settings the three imply 0.8 to 1.6 g of ozone per gram of resist (checks table below). The default is 1.0 g/g. For your own resist, enter a measured mean strip rate and press "Calibrate to this rate".

Validation against published measurements

The checks further down compare the model with exact solutions and with the values it was built from. Figure 7 compares it with published measurements that were not used to build it.

  • Solubility (a). Nine measurements of Henry's constant since 1950, as compiled by Sander (2023). The value used is 0.91 times the median measurement, and eight of the nine lie within 20 %. The measured temperature coefficients are smaller (median 2300 K against 2800 K), so the model gives a little too much ozone in cold water and too little in hot water.
  • Life of ozone (b). Tool makers quote half-lives of about 30 min at 15 °C and 15 min at 25 °C for pH 7 (MKS data sheet; the ACM patent US 12,334,367 uses 15 min for baths of 90 ppm). The rate law gives these values for 5 mg/L, or for 90 mg/L when CO₂ brings the water to pH 4.5; at 90 mg/L and pH 7 it gives under a minute. Dissolvers add CO₂ (MKS uses it as a dopant), which fits, but the quoted values do not state their concentration, and the rate law also shortens faster with temperature than they do. This is the largest open question; stage 1 of the test plan measures it.
  • Strip rate (c). The yield is fitted to two of the three sources and the third is predicted: each comes out within a factor of 1.6.
  • Heated wafer. With cold ozone water on a heated wafer the NEC patent reports 2 and 3 times the rate without heating; the model with the whole film at 60 and 80 °C gives 1.8 and 2.2 times. The direction is right and the gain smaller, as expected from a model with one temperature across the film.
  • Film. Stafford et al. (2024) find, by direct simulation and experiment, that the mean film thickness follows the formula used here, but that the film carries waves when Qν/(ω²r⁵) is below about 10⁻⁷. At the default settings it is 3.4 × 10⁻⁹ at mid-radius, so the real film is wavy, with peaks up to twice the mean. Measured gas-liquid and liquid-solid transfer on spinning discs is higher than for a smooth film (Aoune and Ramshaw 1999; Burns and Jachuck 2005; Zhao 2020), so the transfer in the model is a lower bound; the fitted yield takes up part of the difference.

Test rig

The model can be checked on a bench built around one spin chamber (Figure 8). The rig measures every number the model uses or predicts: the ozone in the generator gas; the dissolved ozone after the contactor, before the nozzle and in the drain; the ozone that escapes into the exhaust; the temperatures, flows and spin speed; and the resist thickness before and after each run. A production tool needs few of these sensors. The rig needs them all, so that each part of the model can be tested on its own and a disagreement points to one part.

Ozone gas from the generator dissolves in a membrane contactor. A back-pressure regulator on the gas outlet sets the contactor pressure, and a chiller sets the water temperature there. The water then passes a flow controller, a heater that sets the process temperature, a bubble trap and a supply line of known length on its way to a fixed nozzle above the centre of the wafer. A three-way valve sends it to the drain until the ozone reading before the nozzle is steady. Ozone gas can also be fed into the chamber lid, which is the "process gas" setting of the model. Everything that can carry ozone gas ends in the destruct unit.

The circles are instruments: AIT-102 measures the ozone in the generator gas, AIT-202 the resistivity of the water (which gives its pH), AIT-204 and AIT-207 the dissolved ozone after the contactor and before the nozzle, AIT-302 the dissolved ozone in the drain, FT-303 and AIT-304 the flow and ozone in the exhaust, and AIT-401 the ozone in the room.

Measurements

Dissolved ozone is read by UV absorption near 260 nm, where ozone in water absorbs with 3000 L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ (Levanov et al. 2016). The cell path is chosen so the absorbance stays between about 0.1 and 1; the sizing table gives it for a 1 mm cell. The monitors are checked against the indigo method (Bader and Hoigné 1981), a wet-chemical method made for ozone in water.

pH. Glass electrodes drift in ultrapure water, so the rig reads resistivity, corrected to 25 °C as such meters do, and converts it to pH with the CO₂ balance of the Chemistry section. Ultrapure water reads 18.2 MΩ cm (Light et al. 2005), water in contact with air about 1 MΩ cm (pH 5.6). The sizing table gives the reading for the pH in the form.

Strip rate. The resist is measured at the same 49 points before and after a run. The clock starts when the ozonated water reaches the wafer and stops with a rinse in ozone-free water. The change in thickness over the time gives the strip rate at each radius, to compare with Figure 1.

Ozone budget. The ozone at the nozzle (AIT-207) times the flow is what goes in. The ozone in the drain (AIT-302) times the flow is what runs off the edge; it is a lower bound, because the droplets lose some ozone in the bowl. The exhaust flow times the ozone in it (FT-303 and AIT-304) is what escapes to the gas, after the background without water is taken off. Runs with and without resist then give the share that reaches the resist, so each bar of Figure 2 can be measured.

Solubility should agree with Henry's law within about 20 %, the scatter of the published measurements. In stage 3 only one number is fitted, the yield, and only at the centre point; the model then has to predict the other runs. A fair target is the mean strip rate within about 20 % and the same order of the effects. If the mean is right but the profile is not, the film flow or the gas exchange is at fault. If twice the ozone does not give twice the rate, the resist is not transport-limited, and a surface rate constant has to be fitted as well (the Python package has one).

Stage 3 is a two-level design in five factors. Sixteen runs, half of the full design (resolution V), separate all five effects and all interactions between two factors; four centre points show curvature and give the repeatability. In these runs the contactor works at the process temperature, so the water at the nozzle is not supersaturated; the sweep with cold-dissolved ozone follows separately. Run the rows in random order. Stop each run before the fastest point clears, so the whole profile can be measured: the last column gives 60 % of the predicted time for the fastest point outside the nozzle zone. The predictions take every other setting from the form.

Safety and materials

  • Parts that touch ozone water are made of PFA, PTFE or PVDF, with seals of perfluoroelastomer (FFKM); many other plastics and elastomers degrade in concentrated ozone. Gas lines are PFA or electropolished stainless steel, cleaned for oxygen service, with no oil or grease.
  • A check valve, a liquid trap and a relief valve keep water and pressure away from the generator.
  • All ozone gas goes to the destruct unit: the contactor off-gas, the bubble-trap vent and the chamber exhaust. The drain water still carries ozone (sizing table), so it needs time to decay, or a vented tank, before it reaches the plant drain.
  • Ambient monitors sit at the chamber and at the generator. The interlock (I in Figure 8) stops the generator and shuts the valve to the nozzle at 0.1 ppm, which is the OSHA limit averaged over 8 h and the NIOSH ceiling, and also on low exhaust flow, an open lid, a leak in the drip tray or lost cooling water. Before the lid opens, the chamber is purged until AIT-304 reads close to zero.
  • Equipment for fabs is assessed against SEMI S2, the environmental, health and safety guideline for semiconductor manufacturing equipment; a laboratory rig should follow the same ideas.

Mechanical design

The rig fits a frame of aluminium profile, 1600 mm wide, 800 mm deep and 2000 mm high, in three bays (Figures 9 and 10). The gas bay on the left holds the ozone generator and its analyser on the upper deck, the mass flow controllers, and the control cabinet with the interlock relay. The water bay in the middle holds the contactor, the heat exchanger, the flow controller, the heater, the bubble trap and the dissolved-ozone monitors over a drip tray with a leak sensor, with the chiller below. The process bay on the right holds the spin chamber with the wafer at 1150 mm, the drain tank below it, and the destruct unit on top of the frame in the exhaust duct. The CAD model (CadQuery) uses the same dimensions as the drawings and is in the repository as STEP files.

The spin module (Figures 11 and 12) is a PVDF chamber with a hinged lid. A catch cup of 380 mm with an inward lip collects the spray; its floor carries the exhaust openings and, behind a weir, the gutter that drains to the drain tank. The vacuum chuck sits on a stainless steel shaft in a PFA sleeve, carried by two bearings in a housing on the deck. Where the shaft enters the chamber, a rotating skirt over a fixed collar forms a labyrinth that is purged with nitrogen, so no seal rubs in ozone water and the motor below stays dry. The nozzle arm holds the PFA nozzle tube 15 mm above the centre of the wafer, adjustable from 10 to 20 mm; a megasonic nozzle on its own arm is an option. The lid has a quartz viewport for the film-thickness sensor and the inlet for ozone gas through a PTFE diffuser.

What is new in the rig

The parts are standard, and ozone water on spinning wafers has been in use since the late 1990s. In the papers and patents we checked we did not find a rig built to close the ozone balance on a spinning wafer, with every measurement tied to one term of a model, nor a test plan that uses the model to pick the runs and to stop each one before the resist clears. Those two things are new here; the hardware is not.

A closed-loop machine

The test rig is built to measure; a machine for production should use what the model shows. In the open rig at its default settings the generator makes 3.2 g of ozone per wafer and 3 % of it ends up in the resist: 83 % of the generator gas leaves the contactor undissolved, and of the ozone that does dissolve, more than a third escapes into the air above the film. The design below closes these losses with four loops and takes its operating point from the model.

  • Ozone cascade. The off-gas of the contactor still carries most of the ozone. Instead of going straight to the destruct unit, it flows through the annulus of a coaxial dispenser into a 3 mm gap under a quartz cover, so the film is covered by ozone-rich gas instead of room air, and it leaves through the gas plenum of the rim collector.
  • Oxygen loop. Behind the destruct unit the gas is almost pure oxygen with some CO₂ and water vapour. It is dried and returned to the generator, and a bleed keeps impurities down. Units for ozone plants recover at least 60 % of the oxygen in this way (Linde, OZORA).
  • Heat recovery and water reclaim. The warm drain heats the ozone water on its way to the nozzle in a counterflow PFA exchanger. With no acid in the process, the drain holds only ozone, CO₂ and the small organic acids from the resist; after ozone removal and a check of TOC and conductivity it goes back to the ultrapure-water plant, as high-volume fabs already do with ozone water (Solid State Phenomena 346, 2023).
  • Supply and operating point. The water is chilled to 10 °C before the contactor, CO₂ in the generator gas brings it to pH 4.5, and the line to the nozzle is 1 m of 4 mm tube with the heater next to the nozzle, following the Tohoku and Nomura patents (preferably no more than 6 s and 5 m from dissolution to use). The operating point was chosen by the model from 1800 settings (Figure 14).

Figure 13. Piping and instruments of the closed-loop machine. The blue labels name the loops. AIT-505 (ozone and CO₂ in the cover gas) and AIT-606 (ozone in the drain) give the end point of the strip; AIT-607 decides between reclaim and drain.

Operating point and use per wafer

The strip of 1 µm of resist on a 300 mm wafer until the slowest point is clear, plus a rinse of 30 s. For single-wafer SPM only the chemicals are given (ACM Research). The assumptions are listed at the end of this section.

Where the gain comes from

Most of the gain comes from the supply and the operating point: cold water with CO₂ at about 100 mg/L that reaches the wafer within a second, and a thinner film from the faster spin. The cover with the off-gas shortens the strip by a further 8 %; it is also what makes the small exhaust and the oxygen loop possible. The generator runs at 1 NL/min, not 3: that takes 9 % longer but makes 64 % less ozone. Two ideas were tried in the model and dropped. Megasonics make the slowest point slower here: they speed the uptake near the centre, which leaves less ozone for the edge. Dussault et al. also saw a lower rate with more megasonic power, though in a different set-up. A second ring of nozzles at 50 mm shortens the strip by 16 % but needs 22 % more water.

Figure 14. Every setting the model tried for the machine: contactor at 10 or 20 °C, pH 4.5 or 5.6, generator gas 1 to 3 NL/min, flow 0.5 to 2 L/min, 25 to 50 °C and 300 to 1600 rpm. Settings are ruled out when the water at the nozzle is more than twice saturated, the film at the edge is thinner than 20 µm, the cover gas loses more than 30 % of its ozone, or inertia is not small. The red dot is the point chosen: within 15 % of the fastest, with the least ozone.

The process module

The module (Figures 15 and 16) keeps the open rig's spin chamber small and closes it over the wafer. A quartz cover, 340 mm across and 12 mm thick, rests in a PVDF carrier ring 3 mm above the wafer while the resist is stripped, and three cylinders on a common plate lift it by 60 mm to change the wafer. A coaxial dispenser through the centre of the cover brings the ozone water in its PFA core and the cover gas in the annulus around it, so both start together at the centre and move outward together. At the rim, the spray from the wafer edge runs under a drip lip onto the outer wall and down into the gutter, while the gas turns up past the lip into the plenum and out through two ports. The water and the gas leave separately, so the drain carries no gas and the gas line no water. The chuck sits on a direct-drive hollow-shaft motor with an encoder, with no belt; the shaft carries the vacuum through a rotary union, and a labyrinth purged with nitrogen keeps the motor dry.

Further details

  • End point. When the resist is gone the ozone in the drain rises and the CO₂ in the cover gas falls. At the operating point the model gives in the drain, so AIT-606 sees a clear step and the strip stops on time instead of after a fixed safety margin. SCREEN reads an end point from the ozone and CO₂ in the exhaust of an ozone-gas strip (US 11,342,162); here the same idea is applied to ozone water.
  • UV without mercury (option). Light below about 190 nm is absorbed in a fraction of a millimetre of water (172 nm within about 0.04 mm; Heit and Braun 1997), so it acts on the water rather than on the resist beneath. At 222 nm and above a film of 0.03 to 0.1 mm is practically transparent (Quickenden and Irvin 1980), so the light reaches the resist. KrCl lamps (222 nm) or UVC LEDs above the quartz avoid mercury, which the EU is phasing out. Whether UV helps the strip is open; stage 4 of the test plan can test it in the rig.
  • Particles (option). Ozone water does not lift particles. With the cover lowered until it touches the liquid, the gap fills, and a transducer on the cover can couple megasonics into hydrogen water for particle removal in the same chamber.
  • Control. The model runs on the controller: it predicts the drain and cover-gas readings for the measured ozone at the nozzle, and a mismatch flags a fault (a clogged nozzle, a weak generator, a leaking cover) before wafers are lost.

Risks, and how the rig tests them

  • Bubbles. The water at the nozzle is 1.7 times saturated (the limit was set at 2). Stage 1 measures how much ozone is lost between the nozzle and the drain, with the bubble trap watched.
  • Film break-up. A film of 27 µm at the edge may break into rivulets on a water-repelling resist. Stage 2 with the film sensor finds the real limit; a lower speed or a little more flow are the fall-backs.
  • Condensation on the cover. The film is warmer than the cover gas, so water can condense on the quartz and drip. The carrier ring is heated to the film temperature and the cover gas is warmed to it.
  • Ozone in the gap. About 5 % ozone by volume sits in the gap. The enclosure is purged, ambient monitors trip the interlock, and the lift does not open until the gap has been purged.
  • The model. The decay law at 100 mg/L is the main open question (Figure 7b), and the smooth-film transfer is a lower bound. Both are measured with the rig before a machine is built.

What is new in the machine

Each part has been done before: ozone gas over a thin water film (Bergman and Hess, US 6,267,125), a plate with a centre nozzle close above the wafer (SCREEN, US 10,141,206), reuse of dissolver off-gas (Liou, US 7,501,055; MKS, US 9,597,613), oxygen recovery for ozone plants (Linde), hot ozone water with a short line (Tohoku University and Nomura Micro Science), an end point from ozone and CO₂ in the exhaust of an ozone-gas strip (SCREEN, US 11,342,162), and UV through a liquid film (SUSS MicroTec Photomask Equipment, US 11,358,172). In the sources we checked we did not find them combined in one tool: the contactor off-gas fed under a quartz cover as the gas phase of the film, an oxygen loop on a wafer tool, an end point from the step in drain ozone that a film model predicts, and an operating point chosen by a model under limits for bubbles, film thickness and cover gas. That combination, and the numbers behind it, is what this design adds. This is not a patent search; one would be needed before any claim.

Assumptions

Checks

The Python version of this model (in the repository) runs the same checks as tests.

Limitations

  • The film is a steady, smooth, laminar film; inertia near the centre and waves are left out, and the zone under the nozzle (r < 10 mm) is not modelled.
  • Water and wafer are at one temperature; a heated wafer under cooler water is not modelled.
  • The nozzle is fixed at the centre; swinging nozzles, sprays and immersion are not modelled.
  • The decay law covers pH 2 to 8 and about 10 to 70 °C; organic acids from the resist, which can scavenge radicals, are ignored.
  • One calibrated yield describes the resist; implanted or hard-baked resists strip differently, and no surface reaction limit is applied by default.
  • The contactor efficiency is set by hand; bubble formation in supersaturated water is flagged but not modelled.
  • UV-assisted ozone and peroxide chemistry is not included.
  • The decay law at the 80 to 100 mg/L used in tools gives much shorter half-lives at pH 7 than tool makers quote (Figure 7b).
  • The closed-loop machine is a design. Its numbers come from the model and from the assumptions listed with it; nothing of it has been built or tested. The second ring of nozzles is in the Python package only.
